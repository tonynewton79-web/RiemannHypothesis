\section{Spatial comparison and the remaining zero-separation problem}
\label{sec:spatialheat}
The height-independent Laguerre comparison is contradicted by the
known density of zeros of $H_{1/2}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:heatgapobstruction}). The proposed spatial
comparison can be sharpened: no particular logarithmic-square
height bound is needed for its maximum-principle consequence.

\begin{theorem}[Spatial parabolic comparison]
\label{thm:spatialmax}
Let $T>0$, $U\in C^{1,2}((0,T]\times\mathbb R)
\cap C([0,T]\times\mathbb R)$ be bounded, and let
$c:[0,T]\times\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)$ be locally bounded.
If
\begin{equation}
U_s-\tfrac12U_{xx}+c(s,x)U\ge0,\qquad
U(0,x)\ge0, \label{eq:spatialmax}
\end{equation}
then $U(s,x)\ge0$ throughout the slab.
No uniform upper bound on $c$ as $|x|\to\infty$
is necessary.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $\varepsilon>0$ put
$W=U+\varepsilon e^s\cosh x$.
Since $U$ is bounded, $W$ is positive
on the spatial boundary of a sufficiently
large rectangle $[0,T]\times[-R,R]$.
It is positive on its initial face. Moreover
\[
(\partial_s-\tfrac12\partial_x^2+c)W
\ge\varepsilon e^s(\tfrac12+c)\cosh x>0.
\]
A negative minimum at an interior spatial
point and positive time would have
$W_s\le0$, $W_{xx}\ge0$, $cW\le0$,
contradicting this strict inequality.
Hence $W\ge0$, and letting
$\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the result.
The argument uses only local bounds for $c$
on each compact rectangle.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Precise sufficient condition for RH]
\label{cor:spatialRH}
For the genuine Riemann heat flow put
$U_n(s,x)=L_{n,\frac12-s}(x)$,
$0\le s\le\frac12$, $n\ge1$.
Suppose there exist nonnegative
locally bounded $c_n(s,x)$ with
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
(n+1)(2n+1)U_{n+1}(s,x)
\le c_n(s,x)U_n(s,x)}
\label{eq:spatialheatgate}
\end{equation}
for every $(s,x)$ and every $n$.
Then every $U_n(s,x)\ge0$, and RH holds.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The genuine heat Fourier kernels form a
uniformly smooth, rapidly decreasing family
on the compact time slab. Each $U_n$ is
bounded and satisfies the backward hierarchy
\eqref{eq:backwardlaguerre}.
Inequality~\eqref{eq:spatialheatgate} gives
$U_{n,s}-\frac12U_{n,xx}+c_nU_n\ge0$.
At $s=0$, $H_{1/2}$ has only real zeros,
hence all generalized Laguerre expressions
are nonnegative. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:spatialmax} separately
to every $n$. The classical
Laguerre--P\'olya characterization
identifies all-order nonnegativity at
$s=1/2$ with the real-zero property of $H_0$.
\end{proof}

The central missing assertion is
\emph{the existence of the locally bounded
coefficients satisfying
\eqref{eq:spatialheatgate}}, not their
global growth rate. In particular, a
logarithmic-square envelope is a candidate
for further analytic work, not a requirement
of the preceding comparison proof.

\begin{proposition}[Exact inverse-gap cost at a simple real root]
\label{prop:gapspatial}
For $G(z)=C\prod_{j=1}^d(z-\rho_j)$
with distinct real roots, fix a root $a$.
Write $b_j=(a-\rho_j)^{-2}$ for $\rho_j\ne a$
and let $e_k$ be their elementary
symmetric functions, $e_0=1$.
Then for $1\le n\le d$,
\begin{equation}
L_n[G](a)=G'(a)^2 e_{n-1}.
\label{eq:gapelementary}
\end{equation}
For $e_{n-1}>0$,
\begin{equation}
0\le
(n+1)(2n+1)\frac{L_{n+1}[G](a)}{L_n[G](a)}
\le\frac{(n+1)(2n+1)}{n}
\sum_{\rho_j\ne a}\frac1{(a-\rho_j)^2}.
\label{eq:gapspatial}
\end{equation}
For $n=1$ the ratio is exactly
$6\sum_{\rho_j\ne a}(a-\rho_j)^{-2}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The factorization
\[
G(a+iy)G(a-iy)
=y^2G'(a)^2
\prod_{\rho_j\ne a}
\left(1+\frac{y^2}{(a-\rho_j)^2}\right)
\]
proves~\eqref{eq:gapelementary}.
For any finite nonnegative list,
$ke_k\le(\sum b_j)e_{k-1}$
by counting the selected index in
each monomial. Apply this at $k=n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Collision obstruction]
\label{prop:spatialcollision}
For $G_{a,b}(z)=(z-a)(z-b)$ with $a\ne b$,
\[
\frac{6L_2[G_{a,b}](a)}{L_1[G_{a,b}](a)}
=\frac6{(a-b)^2}.
\]
Thus mere real-rootedness cannot imply any
universal $C\log^2(2+|a|)$ upper envelope:
the root gap can tend to zero at a fixed
bounded $a$. The backward heat polynomial
$H_t(z)=z^2-2t$ satisfies
\[
L_{1,t}(0)=4t,\quad L_{2,t}(0)=1,\quad
\frac{6L_{2,t}(0)}{L_{1,t}(0)}
=\frac3{2t}\quad(t>0).
\]
At $t=0$, no finite $c(0,0)$ satisfies
$6L_{2,0}(0)\le c(0,0)L_{1,0}(0)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For the quadratic $G_{a,b}$,
$L_1(a)=(a-b)^2$ and $L_2(a)=1$.
For $H_t$, direct differentiation gives
$L_1(x)=2x^2+4t$, $L_2(x)=1$.
All displayed conclusions follow.
\end{proof}

The collision polynomial is a control example;
it does not assert a double zero of the
genuine theta heat flow at a specified time.
At a simple root, the necessary spatial cost
depends on inverse-square zero separations.
The established logarithmic-square
\emph{lower} cost along some $H_{1/2}$
zeros (Theorem~\ref{thm:heatgapobstruction})
must not be mistaken for a matching
upper bound. A natural locally finite comparison is analyzed
more sharply below. Its existence turns out to
encode RH together with simplicity; a separate
integral formulation avoids that extra requirement.

\begin{theorem}[Zero density does not control the spatial
Laguerre coefficient]\label{thm:gapdensity}
There exists an even real entire function $G$ in the
Laguerre--P\'olya class, of order one, whose positive
zeros are all simple and satisfy
\[
N_G^+(T)\sim\frac{T}{4\pi}\log T,
\]
but for which
\begin{equation}
\sup_{\substack{x\in\mathbb R\\L_1[G](x)>0}}
\frac{6L_2[G](x)}
{(1+\log^2(2+|x|))L_1[G](x)}
=+\infty.
\label{eq:gapdensityfail}
\end{equation}
Consequently the fixed-time leading zero-counting
asymptotic for $H_{1/2}$ alone cannot establish
a logarithmic-square upper envelope in
\eqref{eq:spatialheatgate}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For sufficiently large integers $k$, put
\[
T_k=\frac{8\pi k}{\log(k+e)},\qquad
\delta_k=2^{-k},\qquad S_k=T_k+\delta_k.
\]
The sequences $T_k<S_k<T_{k+1}$ are strictly
increasing from some finite index onward.
Indeed $T_k$ is eventually increasing and
$T_{k+1}-T_k\asymp1/\log k$, whereas
$\delta_k$ decays exponentially.
Consider the uniformly convergent product
\[
G(z)=\prod_{k\ge k_0}
\left(1-\frac{z^2}{T_k^2}\right)
\left(1-\frac{z^2}{S_k^2}\right).
\]
Uniform convergence on compact sets follows from
\[
\sum_{k\ge k_0}
(T_k^{-2}+S_k^{-2})<\infty,
\]
since $T_k\asymp k/\log k$.
Every finite partial product has only real zeros,
so $G$ belongs to the Laguerre--P\'olya class.
Its positive zero set consists of the pairs
$T_k,S_k$; all zeros are simple. The exponent
of convergence and counting asymptotic make
its order one, and inversion of
$T_k\sim8\pi k/\log k$ gives
\[
N_G^+(T)\sim
\frac{T\log T}{4\pi}.
\]
At the simple root $x=T_k$, the real-zero
canonical-product formula in
Lemma~\ref{lem:gapratio} yields
\[
\frac{6L_2[G](T_k)}{L_1[G](T_k)}
=6\sum_{\rho\ne T_k}
\frac1{(T_k-\rho)^2}
\ge\frac6{(T_k-S_k)^2}
=6\cdot4^k.
\]
But $\log(2+T_k)=O(\log k)$, so dividing
by $1+\log^2(2+T_k)$ proves
\eqref{eq:gapdensityfail}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Necessary local zero separation]
\label{cor:necessarygap}
Suppose $G$ is a real entire Laguerre--P\'olya
function of order at most one, with simple
real zeros, and for some $C>0$ it satisfies
\[
6L_2[G](x)
\le C\left(1+\log^2(2+|x|)\right)L_1[G](x)
\]
for all real $x$. Then every pair of distinct
real zeros $a,b$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
|a-b|\ge
\frac{\sqrt{6/C}}
{\sqrt{1+\log^2(2+|a|)}}.
\label{eq:necessarygap}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
At the simple zero $a$,
$L_1[G](a)=G'(a)^2>0$.
The canonical-product formula gives
$L_2[G](a)/L_1[G](a)
\ge(a-b)^{-2}$.
Substitute this lower bound into the
hypothesized comparison and rearrange.
\end{proof}

This is a mathematical obstruction to deducing the
proposed logarithmic-square estimate from
\emph{zero density or real-rootedness alone}.
It is not a counterexample to the actual theta
heat flow: the function $G$ is a constructed
Laguerre--P\'olya entire function, not asserted
to arise from the Jacobi kernel. Genuine
theta-specific estimates controlling very close
pairs, or a comparison mechanism that permits
them, are still missing. The exact quadratic
finite-root identity and a sequence of
shrinking-gap tests are reproduced by
\path{certificates/xi_spatial_gap_density_obstruction.py}.
The canonical-product construction and
counting asymptotic are analytic arguments,
not formalized by that finite checker or
the small Lean algebra module.

\paragraph{Quantitative stability under a spatial comparison defect.}
The exact inequality \eqref{eq:spatialheatgate}
is stronger than RH plus the possibility of multiple zeros:
Theorem~\ref{thm:spatialequivalence} identifies
its extra simplicity requirement. A useful
approximate variant avoids dividing by a
Laguerre expression and makes the error budget
of any prospective theta certificate explicit.

\begin{theorem}[Nonnegative-potential defect comparison]
\label{thm:spatialdefect}
Let $0<T<\infty$, and suppose
$U\in C^{1,2}((0,T]\times\mathbb R)
\cap C([0,T]\times\mathbb R)$ is bounded.
Let $c\ge0$ be locally bounded and let
$0\le\varepsilon(s,x)\le\delta$ be a bounded
continuous forcing function. Suppose
\[
U_s-\tfrac12U_{xx}+c(s,x)U\ge-\varepsilon(s,x),
\qquad U(0,x)\ge0.
\]
Then, with the ordinary heat semigroup $P_s$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defectstability}
\boxed{
U(s,x)\ge
-\int_0^s(P_{s-r}\varepsilon(r,\cdot))(x)\,dr
\ge-s\delta.}
\end{equation}
No upper bound for $c(s,x)$ as $|x|\to\infty$
is needed. On a finite rectangle
$[0,T]\times[-R,R]$, if the initial face
is nonnegative and the lateral boundary is
at least $-b$ for some $b\ge0$,
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finiteheatdefect}
U(s,x)\ge-b-s\delta .
\end{equation}
The constant $\delta s$ is optimal even
when $c=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set
\[
E(s,x)=\int_0^s P_{s-r}\varepsilon(r,\cdot)(x)\,dr .
\]
Standard differentiation under the heat kernel
gives $E_s-\tfrac12E_{xx}=\varepsilon$,
$E(0,x)=0$, and $0\le E\le s\delta$.
For $W=U+E$ we have
\[
W_s-\tfrac12W_{xx}+cW
\ge cE\ge0,\qquad W(0,x)\ge0 .
\]
The comparison principle of
Theorem~\ref{thm:spatialmax} yields $W\ge0$,
proving \eqref{eq:defectstability}.
For a rectangle, compare $U$ with
$-b-s\delta$: the difference has
nonnegative parabolic boundary and its
differential inequality acquires the
additional nonnegative term
$c(b+s\delta)$. The ordinary bounded-domain
maximum principle gives
\eqref{eq:finiteheatdefect}. Taking
$U(s,x)=-s\delta$ and $c=0$
gives equality in the full-line bound.
\end{proof}

For the actual theta heat hierarchy,
introduce the \emph{positive part of the
comparison defect}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actualdefect}
\varepsilon_{n,c}(s,x)=
\Bigl[
(n+1)(2n+1)U_{n+1}(s,x)
-c_n(s,x)U_n(s,x)
\Bigr]_+ .
\end{equation}
Whenever this defect is bounded, nonnegative
and continuous, Theorem~\ref{thm:spatialdefect}
gives the quantitative unconditional
\emph{implication}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actualdefectbound}
U_n(s,x)\ge
-\int_0^s
P_{s-r}\varepsilon_{n,c}(r,\cdot)(x)\,dr .
\end{equation}
The word implication matters: a small
bound on the right has not been
established for the genuine theta flow.
In particular, making
$\varepsilon_{n,c}$ small by secretly
assuming $U_n>0$ would be circular.
A genuinely constructive RH proof via
this method must produce coefficients and
a family of error estimates independently
from the square-lattice representation,
with the integrated error tending to zero
for every fixed $n,s,x$.

\begin{proposition}[A persistent defect at a double-root collision]
\label{prop:collisiondefect}
For the backward heat polynomial
$H_t(z)=z^2-2t$ one has
\[
L_{1,t}(x)=2x^2+4t,\qquad L_{2,t}(x)=1.
\]
At the collision $(t,x)=(0,0)$,
for \emph{every finite} $c\ge0$,
\[
6L_{2,0}(0)-cL_{1,0}(0)=6 .
\]
More quantitatively, if $C\ge0$ and
$0\le c\le C$ at a point where
$0\le L_{1,t}(x)\le(1+C)^{-1}$, then
\[
6L_{2,t}(x)-cL_{1,t}(x)\ge5.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The generating product is
\[
H_t(x+iy)H_t(x-iy)
=(x^2-2t)^2+(2x^2+4t)y^2+y^4.
\]
The first two coefficients are the stated
$L_1,L_2$. At the collision the
$L_1$ term vanishes, while $L_2=1$.
The quantitative estimate follows from
$cL_1\le C/(1+C)<1$.
\end{proof}

This supplies an adversarial test for any
proposed \emph{approximately}
spatial comparison. With a coefficient
locally bounded across a multiple-root
collision, the positive defect cannot
be made arbitrarily small. An
unconditionally successful theta-based
proof must either exclude such collisions
from the actual heat slab independently,
or use the zero-compatible
division-free source inequality
\eqref{eq:duhamelmissing}. The latter
remains RH-equivalent and is not yet
certified. Exact rational algebraic
checks for this stability theorem and
its collision model are provided in
\path{XiKernel/SpatialDefectStability.lean}
and
\path{certificates/xi_spatial_defect_stability_exact.py}.
These do not constitute an end-to-end
Lean formalization of the parabolic
maximum principle.

\paragraph{A sharp equivalence, including simplicity.}
The locally finite spatial condition is stronger
than RH. This is not merely a consequence of
the quadratic control example; the obstruction
occurs at every multiple real zero of any
real entire heat transform.

\begin{lemma}[Multiplicity and the adjacent Laguerre orders]
\label{lem:multiplicitylaguerre}
Suppose a real entire function $G$ has a real
zero $a$ of multiplicity $m\ge2$, so that
$G(z)=(z-a)^m g(z)$ with $g(a)\ne0$.
Then
\begin{equation}
L_k[G](a)=0\quad(0\le k<m),\qquad
L_m[G](a)=g(a)^2>0.
\label{eq:multiplicitylaguerre}
\end{equation}
Furthermore, as $h\to0$ through nonzero real
values,
\begin{align}
L_{m-1}[G](a+h)&=
m g(a)^2h^2+O(h^3),\nonumber\\
L_m[G](a+h)&=g(a)^2+O(h),\nonumber\\
m(2m-1)\frac{L_m[G](a+h)}
{L_{m-1}[G](a+h)}
&=\frac{2m-1}{h^2}(1+O(h)).
\label{eq:multipleblowup}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At the multiple zero, the defining
Laguerre generating function is
\[
G(a+iy)G(a-iy)
=y^{2m}g(a+iy)g(a-iy).
\]
Its first nonzero even coefficient is
$g(a)^2$, at degree $2m$,
which proves \eqref{eq:multiplicitylaguerre}.
At $x=a+h$, factor
\[
G(x+iy)G(x-iy)
=(h^2+y^2)^m g(a+h+iy)g(a+h-iy).
\]
The coefficient of $y^{2m-2}$
from the leading constant $g(a)^2$
is $m h^2g(a)^2$.
All other contributions to that
coefficient are $O(h^3)$ or smaller.
The $y^{2m}$ coefficient tends to
$g(a)^2$.
Division proves \eqref{eq:multipleblowup}.
\end{proof}

At $(s,a)$ a multiplicity-$m$ zero gives
$U_{m-1}(s,a)=0$ but $U_m(s,a)>0$.
Thus the right side of
\eqref{eq:spatialheatgate} is zero
for $n=m-1$, while its left side is
strictly positive. No finite coefficient
can satisfy the comparison there.
Equation~\eqref{eq:multipleblowup}
also shows that the required ratio
has an inverse-square singularity
in every punctured neighborhood.

\begin{theorem}[Exact strength of the locally finite
spatial comparison]\label{thm:spatialequivalence}
For the classical Riemann theta heat flow,
the following assertions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every $n\ge1$ there is a
nonnegative, locally bounded
$c_n:[0,\tfrac12]\times\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$
satisfying \eqref{eq:spatialheatgate}
at all points of the closed slab.
\item The Riemann hypothesis holds
\emph{and all nontrivial zeros of the
Riemann zeta function are simple}.
\end{enumerate}
The equivalence uses the classical
de Bruijn--Newman zero dynamics and
the usual genus-one real-zero
canonical product; it is not itself
a proof of either assertion.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Assume (1). The spatial comparison
principle in
Theorem~\ref{thm:spatialmax}
gives $U_n(s,x)\ge0$ for all
$n,s,x$ and therefore RH.
If $H_0$ had a multiple real zero,
Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplicitylaguerre}
would contradict
\eqref{eq:spatialheatgate} at
$s=\tfrac12$. Hence every zero
of $H_0$ is simple, proving (2).

Conversely assume (2). The
Rodgers--Tao theorem gives
$\Lambda\ge0$, while RH gives
$\Lambda\le0$, hence $\Lambda=0$.
At every $t>0=\Lambda$
the classical zero-dynamics theorem
gives real simple zeros of $H_t$;
at $t=0$ this is our assumption.
All these entire transforms
have infinitely many real zeros.
Their order-one real-zero canonical
products show that
$L_{n,t}(x)>0$ for every
$n\ge1$, real $x$ and
$0\le t\le\tfrac12$:
away from a root, the product
$H_t(x+iy)H_t(x-iy)$ has
strictly positive even
coefficients, and at a simple
real root the factor $y^2$
leaves the same positivity at
every positive order.
Thus the quotient
\begin{equation}
c_n(s,x)=
(n+1)(2n+1)\frac{U_{n+1}(s,x)}{U_n(s,x)}
\label{eq:exactlocquotient}
\end{equation}
is well defined, nonnegative
and continuous on the closed slab.
Every compact subset has a
positive minimum for its
denominator, so $c_n$ is
locally bounded and satisfies
\eqref{eq:spatialheatgate} with
equality. This proves (1).
\end{proof}

The converse constructs the coefficient
\emph{from the desired conclusion}.
Consequently \eqref{eq:spatialheatgate},
as a global existence assertion,
is not an independent simplification
of RH: it also requires the still-open
simplicity conjecture. A proof that
begins by assuming positive Laguerre
denominators would therefore be circular.

\paragraph{A zero-compatible, division-free alternative.}
A more natural necessary-and-sufficient
target keeps the coupled source inside
the spatial heat semigroup. Let
$P_s f(x)=\int_{\mathbb R}
(2\pi s)^{-1/2}
e^{-(x-y)^2/(2s)}f(y)\,dy$,
for $s>0$, with $P_0$ the identity.
The mild solution of
\eqref{eq:backwardlaguerre} satisfies
the exact Duhamel formula
\begin{equation}
U_n(s)=P_sU_n(0)-
(n+1)(2n+1)\int_0^s
P_{s-r}U_{n+1}(r)\,dr.
\label{eq:duhamellaguerre}
\end{equation}
The theta transforms and their
derivatives have sufficient
real-axis decay to justify this
representation on the finite slab.
For fixed $n$, the division-free
condition
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
(n+1)(2n+1)\int_0^s
P_{s-r}U_{n+1}(r,x)\,dr
\le(P_sU_n(0))(x)
}
\label{eq:duhamelmissing}
\end{equation}
is \emph{exactly equivalent} to
$U_n(s,x)\ge0$.
Requiring it for all $n,s,x$ is
therefore equivalent to RH, by
the classical Laguerre--P\'olya
criterion and the forward
de Bruijn real-zero theorem.
Unlike \eqref{eq:spatialheatgate},
it does \emph{not} force simplicity:
a legitimate equality at a
multiple zero is permitted.

For example, the exactly solvable
backward heat polynomial
$H_t(z)=z^2-2t$ has
$U_1(s,x)=2x^2+4(t_0-s)$ and
$U_2\equiv1$ if we start at
$t_0>0$. Since
\[
P_sU_1(0,x)=2(x^2+s)+4t_0,
\qquad 6\int_0^sP_{s-r}U_2\,dr=6s,
\]
the Duhamel inequality holds
for $0\le s\le t_0$ and
becomes an equality at
$(s,x)=(t_0,0)$.
At that point the polynomial
has a double real zero and
\eqref{eq:spatialheatgate}
cannot hold for finite $c_1$.
Thus the integral condition
avoids the artificial simplicity
requirement.

The Duhamel criterion remains as
hard as a global Laguerre proof;
it is an equivalent reformulation,
not a claimed advance toward
proving RH. Its advantage is
a better mathematical target
for independent upper estimates
on the \emph{integrated} theta
heat source, with no ratios
at vanishing denominators.
The next genuinely new estimate
must bound this source from the
theta-series structure without
using the sought positivity
as an assumption.

\paragraph{The Duhamel source in two-frequency coordinates.}
The proposed integral comparison admits an exact
spectral formula for the genuine theta heat flow.
Let
\[
d\nu(u)=\tfrac12e^{u^2/2}\Phi(u)\,du,
\qquad a=u+v,\quad b=u-v.
\]
Then $H_{\frac12-s}(x)=
\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-su^2}e^{ixu}\,d\nu(u)$,
and the Laguerre coefficients satisfy
\begin{equation}
U_n(s,x)=\frac1{(2n)!}\iint b^{2n}
e^{-s(u^2+v^2)}e^{ixa}\,d\nu(u)d\nu(v).
\label{eq:duhamelspectralUn}
\end{equation}
The imaginary part vanishes by symmetry.
Writing $\mathcal S_n$ for the integrated
source on the left of \eqref{eq:duhamelmissing},
one has the identity
\begin{align}
\mathcal S_n(s,x)
={}&\frac1{(2n)!}\iint b^{2n}e^{-sa^2/2}\nonumber\\
&\qquad\cdot(1-e^{-sb^2/2})
e^{ixa}\,d\nu(u)d\nu(v).
\label{eq:duhamelspectralsource}
\end{align}

\begin{proposition}[Exact spectral source
decomposition]\label{prop:duhamelspectral}
The formulas above hold for the actual theta
heat flow at every $s\ge0$, real $x$ and
integer $n\ge0$. Before Fourier inversion,
the source is bounded pointwise by its
semigroup budget because
$0\le1-e^{-sb^2/2}\le1$.
This does not, however, imply
$\mathcal S_n(s,x)\le P_sU_n(0,x)$
for every $x$. At $x=0$, that
inequality is strict for $n\ge1$, $s>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expand the product
$H_{\frac12-s}(x+iy)H_{\frac12-s}(x-iy)$
and take the coefficient of $y^{2n}$.
The semigroup $P_\tau$ multiplies
$e^{ixa}$ by $e^{-\tau a^2/2}$.
The factorial identity
\[
\frac{(n+1)(2n+1)}{(2n+2)!}
=\frac1{2(2n)!}
\]
and integration in $r$ give
\[
\frac{b^2}{2}\int_0^s e^{-rb^2/2}\,dr
=1-e^{-sb^2/2}.
\]
Subtracting source from budget leaves
$e^{-s(a^2+b^2)/2}$ in the
double integral, exactly $U_n(s,x)$.
At $x=0$ this residual is strictly
positive, since the theta spectral
density is positive off the diagonal.
At general $x$, $\cos(xa)$ changes
sign, destroying the inference from
pointwise spectral order.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exact source-budget
failure inside the backward heat slab]
\label{prop:atomicduhamel}
Consider the positive even spectral measure
\[
d\nu_{\rm at}=\delta_0+
\tfrac34\delta_{-1}+\tfrac34\delta_1.
\]
Its backward heat Fourier transform is
$G_s(x)=1+d_s\cos x$, where
$d_s=\tfrac32e^{-s}$.
At $s=0$ its zeros are all real.
Nevertheless, at $s=\log(8/5)<1/2$,
\begin{equation}
U_1(s,\pi)=-\frac{15}{256}.
\label{eq:atomicnegative}
\end{equation}
Thus the integrated source exceeds its
semigroup budget by exactly $15/256$.
At the collision time $s_*=\log(3/2)<1/2$,
$U_1(s_*,\pi)=0$ and $U_2(s_*,\pi)=1/4$.
The strict source failure persists for
sufficiently narrow positive Gaussian
mollifications of the atomic measure.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Differentiation gives
\[
U_1(s,x)=d_s(d_s+\cos x),\quad
U_2(s,x)=d_s(4d_s+\cos x)/12.
\]
At $s=\log(8/5)$, $d_s=15/16$;
at $s_*$, $d_s=1$. The
displayed values follow by exact
rational arithmetic. The first time
is less than $1/2$ since
$e^{1/2}>1+\frac12+\frac18>8/5$.
Duhamel's identity says
source minus budget equals $-U_1$.
The initial equation $\cos z=-2/3$
has only real solutions.
Gaussian convolution yields an
even positive rapidly decaying
spectral density; dominated
convergence preserves the strict
negative value for sufficiently
small convolution width.
\end{proof}

These calculations expose a genuine
obstacle but do not disprove the proposed
inequality for the Riemann theta kernel.
The Duhamel inequality remains
RH-equivalent for that kernel, rather
than following from elementary source
positivity.

\paragraph{The Gaussian-mixture proposal fails
for the true theta correlations.}
The residual correlation has the positive density
\begin{align}
\mathcal G_{n,s}(w)
={}&\frac{e^{-sw^2/2}}{2(2n)!}
\int_{\mathbb R}b^{2n}e^{-sb^2/2}\nonumber\\
&\quad\cdot\nu((w+b)/2)\nu((w-b)/2)\,db,
\label{eq:duhamelcorrelation}
\end{align}
whose Fourier transform is $U_n(s,x)$.
Positivity of this density does not
establish Fourier positivity, and
there is a precise obstruction to
one stronger proposed factorization.

\begin{theorem}[No positive Gaussian mixture
for theta correlations]
\label{thm:nogaussianmixture}
For every $n\ge0$ and
$0\le s\le1/2$, the genuine theta
correlation $\mathcal G_{n,s}$ is
strictly positive and decays faster
than every fixed Gaussian:
\begin{equation}
\forall A>0\ \exists C_{A,n,s}<\infty:
\quad
0<\mathcal G_{n,s}(w)
\le C_{A,n,s}e^{-Aw^2}
\quad(w\in\mathbb R).
\label{eq:supergaussiancorrelation}
\end{equation}
Consequently there exists no
nonzero positive measure $\rho$
on $(0,\infty)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G_{n,s}(w)=
\int_{(0,\infty)}e^{-\lambda w^2}
\,d\rho(\lambda)
\quad(w\in\mathbb R).
\label{eq:impossiblemixture}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By evenness and the defining
square-lattice theta series, for
$u\ge0$ one has
\[
0<\Phi(u)\le
C e^{9u-\pi e^{4u}}.
\]
Indeed $e^{4u}\ge1$ and the
remaining sum of
$m^4e^{-\pi(m^2-1)}$
is finite. Hence
$\nu(u)=\frac12e^{u^2/2}\Phi(u)$
decays faster than any Gaussian:
for each $A>0$ a constant $K_A$
satisfies
$0<\nu(u)\le K_Ae^{-Au^2}$
for all real $u$.
Using $(w+b)^2+(w-b)^2
=2(w^2+b^2)$ in
\eqref{eq:duhamelcorrelation} gives
\[
\mathcal G_{n,s}(w)
\le\frac{K_A^2}{2(2n)!}
e^{-Aw^2/2}
\int_{\mathbb R}
b^{2n}e^{-Ab^2/2}\,db.
\]
The parameter $A$ is arbitrary,
so replacing it by $2A$
establishes \eqref{eq:supergaussiancorrelation}.
The density is strictly positive
because its integrand is positive
off $b=0$.

Suppose \eqref{eq:impossiblemixture}
held. At $w=0$ the measure is
finite and nonzero, since
$\mathcal G_{n,s}(0)>0$.
There must be a finite $L>0$
with $m:=\rho((0,L])>0$.
Then for all real $w$,
\[
\mathcal G_{n,s}(w)
\ge m e^{-Lw^2}.
\]
Taking $A>L$ in
\eqref{eq:supergaussiancorrelation}
would require
$m e^{-Lw^2}\le
C_Ae^{-Aw^2}$ for every $w$,
which is impossible as
$|w|\to\infty$.
\end{proof}

This excludes \emph{positive mixtures
of centred Gaussians}, not positive
definiteness itself. Positive-definite
functions may have super-Gaussian
decay; for example autocorrelations
of compactly supported smooth
functions are positive definite.
The remaining theta-specific
task is therefore a different,
constructive Fourier-positive
representation, such as an explicit
sum of convolution squares.
Its existence is unproved
and an abstract existence assertion
would again be equivalent to
the missing Laguerre inequalities.
