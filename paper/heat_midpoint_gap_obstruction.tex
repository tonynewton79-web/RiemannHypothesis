\paragraph{An inverse-gap obstruction at the midpoint of two zeros.}
\label{par:midpoint-spatial}
The coefficient needed by the backward heat inequality
can be forced to be large not only at a zero of the initial
real-rooted transform, but between adjacent zeros.
This gives a direct and sharp lower bound from one
gap, independent of the rest of the zero set.

\begin{theorem}[Exact midpoint inverse-gap bound]
\label{thm:midpointgap}
Let $G$ be a real entire Laguerre--P\'olya function
of genus at most one with at least two distinct
real zeros. Let $a<b$ be adjacent simple zeros,
put $m=(a+b)/2$ and $g=b-a>0$.
Then $G(m)\ne0$, $L_1[G](m)>0$, and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\quad
6\frac{L_2[G](m)}{L_1[G](m)}
\ \ge\ \frac{12}{g^2}.
\quad}
\label{eq:midpointgap}
\end{equation}
Equality holds when $G$ is proportional to
$(z-a)(z-b)$ times a zero-free exponential
with no quadratic term.
In particular, whenever $G=H_t$ is
the genuine de Bruijn--Newman transform at a
strictly real-rooted heat time $t>\Lambda$,
every admissible comparison coefficient obeys
\[
c_1(\tfrac12-t,m)\ \ge\ \frac{12}{g^2}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
At $m$, the two selected zeros contribute
the identical positive inverse-square weight
$\lambda=4/g^2$ to the real-zero canonical
product. Let $B\ge0$ be the sum of the
inverse-square weights of all remaining zeros,
and let $E\ge0$ be their second elementary
symmetric sum. Since $G$ has genus at most one,
\[
\frac{G(m+iy)G(m-iy)}{G(m)^2}
=\bigl(1+\lambda y^2\bigr)^2
\prod_{\rho\notin\{a,b\}}
\left(1+\frac{y^2}{(m-\rho)^2}\right).
\]
Equating the first two coefficients of $y^2$
gives
\[
\frac{L_1[G](m)}{G(m)^2}=2\lambda+B,
\qquad
\frac{L_2[G](m)}{G(m)^2}
=\lambda^2+2\lambda B+E.
\]
The denominator is strictly positive, and
\[
6(\lambda^2+2\lambda B+E)
-3\lambda(2\lambda+B)
=9\lambda B+6E\ge0.
\]
Thus $6L_2/L_1\ge3\lambda=12/g^2$,
as claimed. For the pure quadratic factor
$B=E=0$ and equality holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Logarithmic-square envelopes force
a zero-spacing lower bound]
\label{cor:midpointspacing}
Suppose that for some $t>\Lambda$,
$c_1(\tfrac12-t,x)\le C\{1+\log^2(2+|x|)\}$
holds at every real $x$, with $C>0$.
Every adjacent pair $a<b$ of zeros of $H_t$ must obey
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
b-a\ \ge\
\sqrt{\frac{12}{C\left(1+\log^2(2+|(a+b)/2|)\right)}}.
}
\label{eq:midpointspacing}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Combine \eqref{eq:midpointgap} with
the hypothesized upper bound at the midpoint.
All quantities are positive, and rearrange.
\end{proof}

This theorem sharpens the two-root obstruction
at a root (which contributes $6/g^2$).
It does not improve the separate
global logarithmic-square \emph{average}
lower threshold of Theorem~\ref{thm:log-square-necessary}.
In particular, a proposed
$O(\log^2(2+|x|))$ \emph{upper} envelope for
the genuine theta heat flow would entail
nontrivial pointwise lower bounds on
\emph{every} positive-time zero gap.
The known zero-counting asymptotic gives
no such individual lower spacing bound;
one cannot infer the envelope from
density information or the already
established central moment asymptotic.

The coefficient inequality remains
unproved at arbitrary Fourier height.
