\paragraph{No uniform single-anchor step at unbounded height.}
The optimal two-moment estimate above does not merely
lack a verified uniform constant: its single-anchor
criterion necessarily degenerates along a sequence
of genuine theta-flow zero pairs.

\begin{proposition}[Unavoidable high-zero degeneration]
\label{prop:single-anchor-degeneration}
Let $t_*=1/2$, where the genuine
$H_{t_*}$ has only real simple zeros.
Along an unbounded sequence of consecutive
positive zeros $a_i<b_i$, the gaps
$g_i=b_i-a_i$ tend to zero.
For each pair choose a protecting disk of
radius $0<r_i<g_i/2$ around $a_i$, and
let $\Gamma_i$ contain its boundary,
possibly together with the second disk
and the enclosing corridor.
Set
\[
m_i=\inf_{z\in\Gamma_i}|H_{t_*}(z)|,\quad
Y_i=\sup_{z\in\Gamma_i}|\Im z|.
\]
For each fixed $\sigma>0$, the optimal
two-moment upper bound
\[
J_i(\sigma)=A_{Y_i}
(1-e^{-\sigma M_{Y_i}/A_{Y_i}})
\]
eventually exceeds $m_i$:
\[
\boxed{J_i(\sigma)>m_i
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }i.}
\]
Thus a single set of contours anchored at
$t_*=1/2$, with one fixed positive
backward-time step, cannot certify all
genuine theta zero pairs by the Jensen
or Pad\'e modulus test.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The known real-zero density of $H_{1/2}$
grows as $N_{1/2}(T)\sim
T\log T/(4\pi)$; the interval $[T,2T]$
therefore contains adjacent gaps
of length $O(1/\log T)$. Select one
such pair for each large $T$,
with $g_i\to0$.

For large $i$, $r_i<1$ and the
circle around $a_i$ contains
$a_i+i r_i$. Since $H_{t_*}(a_i)=0$,
integrate its derivative on the
vertical segment to obtain
\[
m_i\le|H_{t_*}(a_i+i r_i)|
\le r_i C_1,\quad
C_1=\int_0^\infty
u\Phi(u)e^{t_*u^2+u}\,du<\infty.
\]
Hence $m_i\to0$.

For fixed $\sigma>0$, put
\[
B_0(\sigma)=\int_0^\infty
\Phi(u)e^{t_*u^2}
(1-e^{-\sigma u^2})\,du>0.
\]
The positive-weight integral
$B_{Y_i}(\sigma)$ from the proof
of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-moment-shield}
satisfies $B_{Y_i}(\sigma)\ge B_0(\sigma)$
because $Y_i\ge0$.
Jensen's inequality in that theorem
gives
\[
J_i(\sigma)\ge B_{Y_i}(\sigma)
\ge B_0(\sigma)>0.
\]
Since $m_i\to0$, eventually
$J_i(\sigma)>m_i$ as claimed.
The Pad\'e upper bound is at least
$J_i(\sigma)$, so its contour
criterion also fails for these pairs.
\end{proof}

The result does not assert a collision or
an off-critical Riemann zero.
It identifies a specific obstruction
to the \emph{proof mechanism}.
A genuine all-height continuation argument
would need certified time-dependent contour
recentering with no unbounded-height escape,
or an independent global zero-exclusion theorem.
