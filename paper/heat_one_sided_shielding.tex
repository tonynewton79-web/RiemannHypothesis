\paragraph{A one-sided inverse-square shielding budget.}
\label{par:one-sided-shielding}
The integrated requirement \eqref{eq:integratedshieldu}
does not need two-sided control of a whole neighborhood
of the consecutive pair. There is a sharper geometric
comparison that penalizes \emph{only outward gaps larger
than the gap under protection}. This identifies a
particular observable of the moving zero configuration
whose heat-time integral would suffice.

Suppose $a<b$ are consecutive real simple zeros,
$g=b-a>0$, and enumerate the exterior real zeros
as $a-\ell_m$ and $b+r_m$ $(m\ge1)$, with
$\ell_m,r_m>0$ increasing. Define successive
exterior gaps
\[
d_{L,1}=\ell_1,\quad
d_{L,j}=\ell_j-\ell_{j-1}\ (j\ge2),
\qquad
d_{R,1}=r_1,\quad
d_{R,j}=r_j-r_{j-1}\ (j\ge2).
\]
In particular
$\ell_m=\sum_{j=1}^m d_{L,j}$
and similarly on the right.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp one-sided shielding bound]
\label{thm:onesidedshield}
Assume the bi-infinite real simple zero sequence
has absolutely convergent shielding sum
\[
\mathcal S=\sum_{m\ge1}
\left\{\frac1{\ell_m(\ell_m+g)}
+\frac1{r_m(r_m+g)}\right\}.
\]
Let $D=2-g^2\mathcal S$. Then, for every integer $R\ge1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onesidedfinite}
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
[D]_+ \le{}&\frac2{R+1}\\
&+\sum_{j=1}^R
\left(\frac1{j^2}-\frac1{(R+1)^2}\right)
\frac{
(d_{L,j}-g)_++(d_{R,j}-g)_+}{g}.
\end{aligned}}
\end{equation}
Consequently, in the extended nonnegative real sense,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:onesidedinfinite}
\boxed{
[D]_+\le\frac1g\sum_{j\ge1}
\frac{(d_{L,j}-g)_++(d_{R,j}-g)_+}{j^2}.
}
\end{equation}
These inequalities require no restriction on
outward gaps \emph{smaller} than $g$.
The coefficient $j^{-2}$ is optimal to
first order in a single outward-gap perturbation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $f(v)=1/(v(v+1))$ for $v>0$.
The two normalized shielding contributions
at index $m$ are $f(\ell_m/g)$ and $f(r_m/g)$.
The regular-lattice reference satisfies
$\sum_{m\ge1}f(m)=1$.
Since
\[
-f'(v)=\frac1{v^2}-\frac1{(v+1)^2}
\]
is positive and strictly decreasing,
the mean-value theorem gives, for $v>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent}
f(m)-f(v)\le
\left(\frac1{m^2}-\frac1{(m+1)^2}\right)
(v-m)_+.
\end{equation}
For $v\le m$ the left side is nonpositive;
for $v>m$ integrate $-f'$ between $m$ and $v$.

Omitting all shielding terms with $m>R$ can
only \emph{increase} $D$. Therefore
\begin{align*}
D
&\le 2-\sum_{m=1}^R
\{f(\ell_m/g)+f(r_m/g)\}\\
&=\frac2{R+1}+
\sum_{m=1}^R
\{f(m)-f(\ell_m/g)+f(m)-f(r_m/g)\}.
\end{align*}
Let $\eta_{L,j}=d_{L,j}/g-1$ and similarly
$\eta_{R,j}$. Then
\[
(\ell_m/g-m)_+
=\left(\sum_{j=1}^m\eta_{L,j}\right)_+
\le\sum_{j=1}^m(\eta_{L,j})_+.
\]
Apply \eqref{eq:tangent} on both sides,
interchange two finite nonnegative sums, and use
the exact telescoping identity
\[
\sum_{m=j}^R
\left(\frac1{m^2}-\frac1{(m+1)^2}\right)
=\frac1{j^2}-\frac1{(R+1)^2}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:onesidedfinite};
its right side is nonnegative and therefore
also bounds $[D]_+$.

If the infinite series on the right of
\eqref{eq:onesidedinfinite} diverges there
is nothing further to prove. Otherwise
the finite sums converge to it as $R\to\infty$,
and the first term tends to zero; this proves
the infinite bound.

To see first-order sharpness,
start with a perfect lattice of gap $g$
and enlarge only the $j$th exterior gap on
one side from $g$ to $(1+\delta)g$,
$\delta>0$. Then
\[
D(\delta)=\sum_{m=j}^\infty
\{f(m)-f(m+\delta)\},\qquad
D'(0+)=\sum_{m=j}^\infty
\left(\frac1{m^2}-\frac1{(m+1)^2}\right)
=\frac1{j^2}.
\]
At $\delta=1$ the difference telescopes again:
\[
D(1)=\frac1{j(j+1)}.
\]
Thus the linear inverse-square coefficient
cannot be uniformly lowered even in
an arbitrarily small single-gap perturbation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Conditional one-sided
heat-time collision barrier]\label{cor:onesidedheat}
Along any backward heat interval on which the
zero-motion equation \eqref{eq:shieldgap} holds,
let $d_{L,j}(s),d_{R,j}(s)$ denote the moving
exterior gaps. For a fixed integer $R\ge1$ put
\[
\mathcal E_R(s)=\frac2{R+1}
+\sum_{j=1}^R
\left(\frac1{j^2}-\frac1{(R+1)^2}\right)
\frac{
(d_{L,j}(s)-g(s))_+
+(d_{R,j}(s)-g(s))_+}{g(s)}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-sided-dynamic}
G(s)\ge G(0)-4\int_0^s\mathcal E_R(v)\,dv.
\end{equation}
In particular, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-sided-budget}
\boxed{
4\int_0^\sigma\mathcal E_R(v)\,dv
<G(0)
}
\end{equation}
throughout the real-simple evolution up to
a proposed terminal time $\sigma$,
this chosen pair cannot collide by that time.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:onesidedshield},
$[D(s)]_+\le\mathcal E_R(s)$.
Since $G'=-4D\ge-4[D]_+$,
integration gives \eqref{eq:one-sided-dynamic}.
The strict bound \eqref{eq:one-sided-budget}
forces the gap square to stay positive.
\end{proof}

The improvement is structural, not yet a theorem
about actual Riemann zero rigidity. The earlier
near-lattice criterion constrained both signs of
every exterior displacement. Here only the
\emph{positive excess} of each successive exterior
gap enters, and the weights are inverse-square.
The finite-$R$ remainder $2/(R+1)$ is explicit;
over a half-unit backward interval it alone
contributes $4/(R+1)$ to the total budget.
Thus high zeros with $g(0)\asymp1/\log X$
still call for $R$ on the scale of $(\log X)^2$,
even if all weighted excesses vanished.

The single-gap example also exposes why
zero-density information alone is insufficient:
doubling one exterior gap at index $j$
changes the local shielding deficit by
$1/[j(j+1)]$, despite leaving the rest of
the ideal infinite lattice unchanged.
Moreover, the positive-part budget
\eqref{eq:one-sided-budget} is
\emph{sufficient but not necessary}:
a squared gap may remain positive despite
repeated decreases and recoveries whose total
downward variation exceeds its initial value.

The true missing estimate is now explicit:
establish \eqref{eq:one-sided-budget}, or a
comparably effective signed-deficit integral,
for \emph{all} genuine theta-flow zero pairs
through backward time $1/2$, with a further
argument precluding a first loss of real
rootedness at unbounded height. Neither
assertion follows from the current theta
moment bounds, the local seven-unit
Laguerre interval, or the known average
zero-density formula. Consequently
Theorem~\ref{thm:onesidedshield}
does not establish RH.

\paragraph{An exact nonlinear exterior-gap identity.}
\label{par:exact-gap-shielding}
The positive-part estimate above admits an exact signed
telescoping formula. Besides sharpening the estimate, the
formula determines the backward heat-flow direction of an
extremal gap.

\begin{proposition}[Exact exterior-gap defect]
\label{prop:exactexteriordefect}
Let $a<b$ be consecutive simple real zeros,
$g=b-a>0$, and write each successive left and right
exterior gap as
\[
d_{\nu,m}=g(1+\varepsilon_{\nu,m}),
\quad \nu\in\{L,R\},\quad m\ge1,
\]
where $\varepsilon_{\nu,m}>-1$.
Set $D_{\nu,0}=0$ and
$D_{\nu,m}=\sum_{j=1}^m\varepsilon_{\nu,j}$.
Assume that the exterior zero sequences tend to
$-\infty$ and $+\infty$, respectively.
Then the shielding deficit is exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exactdefect}
\boxed{
2-g^2\mathcal S
=\sum_{\nu\in\{L,R\}}\sum_{m=1}^\infty
\frac{\varepsilon_{\nu,m}}
{(m+D_{\nu,m-1})(m+D_{\nu,m})}.}
\end{equation}
If a corresponding series is not absolutely convergent,
the equality is understood as the ordered outward limit
of its partial sums. Its finite-$R$ form is
\begin{align}
1-\sum_{m=1}^R
\frac1{(m+D_m)(m+1+D_m)}
={}&\sum_{m=1}^R
\frac{\varepsilon_m}
{(m+D_{m-1})(m+D_m)}
\nonumber\\
&+\frac1{R+1+D_R}.
\label{eq:exactdefectfinite}
\end{align}
For a single enlarged exterior gap
$d_{\nu,j}=(1+\delta)g$, with $\delta\ge0$
and all other gaps equal to $g$, its exact
one-sided contribution to the deficit is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:singlegapexact}
\boxed{\Delta_j(\delta)=
\frac{\delta}{j(j+\delta)}.}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For one exterior side, the normalized distance from
the nearer endpoint to the $m$th exterior zero
is $z_m=m+D_m>0$, so its shielding term is
$[z_m(z_m+1)]^{-1}$.
Write $y_m=m+D_{m-1}$; then
$z_m-y_m=\varepsilon_m$ and
$y_{m+1}=z_m+1$.
The elementary identity
\[
\frac1{z_m(z_m+1)}
+\frac{z_m-y_m}{y_mz_m}
=\frac1{y_m}-\frac1{z_m+1}
\]
telescopes over $1\le m\le R$, because
$y_1=1$. This proves
\eqref{eq:exactdefectfinite}.
Since $z_R=\ell_R/g$ (or $r_R/g$)
tends to infinity, the final term tends to zero.
Adding the two sides proves \eqref{eq:exactdefect},
with the stated ordered interpretation.
When only the $j$th outward gap changes,
the shielding loss is
\[
\sum_{m=j}^\infty
\left[\frac1{m(m+1)}
-\frac1{(m+\delta)(m+1+\delta)}\right]
=\frac1j-\frac1{j+\delta},
\]
which equals \eqref{eq:singlegapexact}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Backward anti-diffusion of extremal gaps]
\label{cor:mingapantidiffusion}
On a backward heat-time interval where the
zeros of $H_{\frac12-s}$ remain real and simple,
let $G(s)=g(s)^2$ for a selected consecutive pair.
Its exact evolution can be written
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exactgapflow}
\boxed{
G'(s)=-4\sum_{\nu\in\{L,R\}}\sum_{m\ge1}
\frac{\varepsilon_{\nu,m}(s)}
{(m+D_{\nu,m-1}(s))(m+D_{\nu,m}(s))}.}
\end{equation}
In particular, at any time when $g(s)$ is a
global minimum among all adjacent zero gaps,
$G'(s)\le0$, with strict inequality unless the
entire bilateral zero configuration is an
arithmetic progression with spacing $g(s)$.
If $g(s)$ is a global maximum, then
$G'(s)\ge0$ under the same bilateral assumptions.
These are instantaneous statements, not
global minimum-gap persistence theorems.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Insert \eqref{eq:exactdefect} into
$G'=-8+4G\mathcal S$.
If $g$ is minimal, all exterior
$\varepsilon_{\nu,m}\ge0$; the denominators
are positive. Equality holds only if every
exterior excess vanishes. If $g$ is maximal,
all excesses are nonpositive and the sign reverses.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A finite-neighborhood minimum is insufficient.}
The minimum-gap sign conclusion requires a \emph{global}
minimum; in an infinite zero sequence such a minimum
need not be attained. It cannot be replaced by a
minimum among only the adjacent exterior gaps.
Indeed, take a protected gap $g=1$ and, on each side,
three outward exterior zeros at normalized
distances
\[
z_1=1,\qquad z_2=\frac{101}{100},
\qquad z_3=\frac{102}{100}.
\]
The immediately neighboring exterior gap equals $g$,
but the following exterior gaps are only $g/100$.
Their six shielding contributions already give
\[
g^2\mathcal S\ge
2\left(\frac12+\frac{10000}{20301}
+\frac{10000}{20604}\right)
=\frac{340039}{115039}>2.
\]
Consequently
\[
G'(s)\ge-8+4\frac{340039}{115039}
=\frac{439844}{115039}>0.
\]
Any additional real exterior zeros increase the shielding
sum further. Thus the gap can grow backward despite being
smallest among its immediate neighbors. Both the displayed
fractions and the sign are verified by exact rational
arithmetic. This configuration is a geometric adversarial
test, not an assertion about the actual Riemann zero spacings.

The identity isolates the exact integrated quantity
needed for continuation:
\[
G(s)=G(0)-4\int_0^s
\sum_{\nu,m}
\frac{\varepsilon_{\nu,m}(v)}
{(m+D_{\nu,m-1}(v))(m+D_{\nu,m}(v))}\,dv .
\]
At a shortest gap the integrand is nonnegative.
This observation is a constraint on a proposed
minimum-gap growth argument, not a theorem that
the gap must collide. The difficult, still unproved
step is a theta-specific bound on the accumulated
defect uniformly in the pair, heat time and height.
A first-collision continuation at unbounded height
must also be excluded by a separate compactness argument.
