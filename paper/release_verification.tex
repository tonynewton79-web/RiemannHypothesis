\section{Reproducibility and mathematical scope}
\label{sec:verification-release}
The numerical claims in this paper arise from three
different forms of calculation, and their logical roles
should be kept separate. The fifth-iterate calculation
for the genuine theta kernel uses outward rational
integer intervals, a Machin-series enclosure for
$\pi$, and explicit control of the omitted theta
summands. Its certificate is reproduced by
\path{certificates/xi_fifth_iterate_exact.py}.
The Gaussian-polynomial identities, the
large-frequency truncation coefficients and
the rational bounds on the exponential theta
tail can be checked directly from the
recurrences given in the proofs.

The radial monotone-curvature property of the classical
theta potential was previously proved by Newman in 1991
using a GHS inequality \cite{NewmanGHS}. The certificate
provided here is therefore an alternative quantitative
verification, not the discovery of that property.
The radial-curvature certificate is a
computer-assisted analytic result.
Its proof separates a compact interval,
certified by 500 outward-rounded rational
cells, from a distant region controlled
by an explicit infinite-tail majorant.
The implementation and the relevant
polynomial identities appear in
\path{certificates/xi_global_curvature_exact.py}.
Although the arithmetic checks are exact,
an independent mathematical review of
the complete tail comparison and the
placement of this theorem within earlier
theta-kernel literature remains appropriate.

The four finite zero-count theorems certify
distinct disks. The first three use a two-mode
Fourier approximation; the fourth requires
three modes and tighter local derivative bounds.
Their proofs reduce to enclosures of
three Taylor coefficients and one
positive remainder integral. Every
coefficient is computed at two
different composite-Simpson resolutions;
the stated fourth-derivative bounds
and the omitted $r>2$ integral tails
are handled analytically.
The integration nodes use
\texttt{mpmath.iv} version 1.3.0 for
interval enclosures of $\pi$,
exponentials and trigonometric values,
which are subsequently converted to
exact rational endpoints.
Thus the computational trust boundary
includes that library's transcendental
interval implementation as well as
the elementary analytic estimates.
The scripts, available in the
\texttt{certificates} directory,
may be run independently of the
LaTeX build. The corresponding tests
and their results are recorded
in the public repository's
\texttt{RESULTS.md} file.

The Lean 4.24.0/Mathlib development
certifies exact algebraic identities,
moment-positivity statements, rational
comparison gates, and the functional
symmetry of an entire form of
the classical completed zeta function.
For these small algebraic theorems,
the recorded axiom inspection reports
the ordinary Mathlib axioms
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice},
and \texttt{Quot.sound}.
The large rational-jet test instead
uses \texttt{native\_decide}, which
also relies on compiler-trust axioms;
it is not a small-kernel-only check.
Neither the interval-Simpson
calculations nor the full
Jacobi transformation, Fourier
integrals and Rouch\'e transfers
have been formalized end to end
in Lean.

The existence and simplicity of
the four zeros certified here
are classical results. Indeed,
Platt and Trudgian verified the
critical-line location and
simplicity of all nontrivial
zeta zeros up to height
$3\cdot10^{12}$ \cite{PlattTrudgian}.
The present certificates therefore
make no claim of a new zeta-zero
location or an improvement in
verified height. Their value is
to test the particular theta-Fourier
approximation and its explicit
Rouch\'e error bounds.

The spatial Laguerre comparison has also been
audited at vanishing denominators.
A multiplicity-$m$ zero forces the
$(m-1)$st Laguerre coefficient to
vanish while the next coefficient
is positive; hence the proposed
locally finite pointwise ratio
cannot exist there.
Using the classical real-zero
heat dynamics, the full comparison
is equivalent to RH together
with simplicity, not RH alone.
The proof is developed in
Theorem~\ref{thm:spatialequivalence}.
A division-free Duhamel
comparison avoids the added
simplicity condition but is still
equivalent to global Laguerre
positivity. The new polynomial
identities and saturation test
are independently checked in
\path{certificates/xi_spatial_equivalence_exact.py}.
The Lean files verify finite
algebraic gates only; the full
analytic equivalence and
parabolic semigroup theorem
are not kernel-formalized.

The open difficulty remains global.
The explicit theta truncation estimate
can be made arbitrarily small on the
complex strip, with an adaptive
number of modes growing as
$O(\sqrt{\log R})$ for prescribed
polynomial accuracy at height $R$.
What is unavailable is a
height-uniform strict lower bound
on the approximating transform
over contours that would exclude
off-critical zeros. Nor do the
finite Laguerre tests supply that
missing lower bound, as the
counterexamples in this paper show.
Consequently neither the four
bounded zero counts nor the
formal algebraic results prove
the Riemann hypothesis.

The high-order Duhamel budget theorem added
in the latest revision is a purely analytic
consequence of the genuine positive theta
correlation measure. For fixed positive
backward heat time, its central-frequency
source/budget fraction increases strictly
with Laguerre order and tends to one.
The finite rational covariance identities
and Gaussian reference model are tested in
\path{certificates/xi_duhamel_order_saturation_exact.py};
Lean checks their algebraic subclaims in
\path{XiKernel/DuhamelOrderSaturation.lean}.
No end-to-end Lean proof of the infinite
correlation integral or the all-order
limit is claimed. The theorem rules out
an order-independent fractional slack,
not the original RH-equivalent source
inequality.

The constant-penalty positive-part Duhamel criterion is
a zero-compatible reformulation, not an analytic proof
of positivity. Its strict collision and negative-region
model inequalities are checked exactly by
\path{certificates/xi_penalty_defect_limit_exact.py};
Lean checks the algebraic comparisons in
\path{XiKernel/PenaltyDefectLimit.lean}. The theta
semigroup limit is not formally proved in Lean.


The quantitative normalized-penalty extension sharpens the
previous collision-compatible criterion. Its pointwise
source inequality is independent of RH, and Gaussian
heat averaging gives a spatially uniform
$O(C^{-1})$ error for each fixed Laguerre order
between normalized penalty and the smoothed
negative part. An exact Fraction-based test checks
15,000 source sandwiches and 1,000 finite
positive-averaging examples, together with a sharp
negative-region limit. The associated Lean module
\path{XiKernel/PenaltyQuantitative.lean}
contains algebraic subclaims only; the heat-semigroup
transfer and the RH equivalence are proved analytically
in the main text, not formally checked end to end.
The essential theta-specific bounded-penalty
estimate remains open. The bibliographic record is separate from the computational verification record here.

The complete source, reproducibility
instructions and verification record
are maintained at
\url{https://github.com/tonynewton79-web/RiemannHypothesis}.


The central-observation Duhamel reduction is proved in
\path{paper/central_duhamel_penalty_attack.tex}.
It uses the unconditional positive-part sandwich, strict
positivity of Gaussian convolution and the classical
Laguerre--P\'olya criterion to reduce the RH-equivalent
boundedness test to $x=0$ for all orders.
The negative-witness theorem gives an explicit
Gaussian-suppressed lower bound for any hypothetical
off-axis violation. A further noncommutation theorem,
using the published bound $\Lambda\le0.22$ and the
finite theta-mode endpoint asymptotics, proves that the
large-$C$ penalty limit cannot be taken before removing
any fixed modal cutoff, even on the already known
real-zero heat interval $t\in[1/4,1/2]$.
Local exact Fraction tests passed for 400 witness
rectangles, 36 scalar normalizations and five negative
toy limits. The Lean algebra file
\path{XiKernel/CentralDuhamel.lean} was submitted,
but its latest CI run remained queued at the time of
this revision; it is not described as passing.
Neither the heat integral nor the full RH-equivalent
bound is end-to-end formalized, and the actual
theta-specific all-order bounded penalty estimate
remains unproved.


\paragraph{All-order zero-lattice and extended heat band (20 October).}
The supplementary analysis identifies every Laguerre coefficient
at a simple real zero of $H_{1/2}$ with an elementary symmetric
sum of reciprocal squared zero gaps. It also proves that
a zero of multiplicity $m\ge2$ makes any finite
pointwise heat comparison impossible at Laguerre order $m-1$.
The existing theta moment estimate extends its common
all-order central comparison interval from $|x|\le6$
to $|x|<\sqrt{48}$, with a spatial coefficient depending
only on strictly positive central moments and the rational
factor $(1-x^2/48)^{-1}$.
The exact rational and finite symmetric-polynomial checks
were rerun independently in the local container;
the new CI workflows have not yet left their queued state.
The analytic canonical-product and theta moment inputs
are not formalized end to end in Lean.
The complete source is given in
\path{paper/zero_lattice_extended_band.tex}
and \path{XiKernel/ZeroLatticeAndBand.lean}.
No global RH exclusion has been obtained.


\paragraph{Compact-frequency high-order theorem (20 October 2026).}
A new analytic argument based on concentration of
the positive theta pair measure establishes
$\sup_{0\le t\le1/2}\mathbb E_{n,t}(u+v)^2\to0$.
It follows that for each fixed bounded frequency
interval, all sufficiently high generalized
Laguerre orders are positive uniformly in heat time.
This removes the infinite-order quantifier on
each \emph{fixed} frequency rectangle,
but not on the unbounded real axis.
The proof uses Newman's established monotone
theta-potential curvature, superexponential
tails, a conditional integration-by-parts
variance estimate, and elementary
large-separation concentration.
The accompanying Lean module
\path{XiKernel/HighOrderCompactPositivity.lean}
checks only finite algebraic inequalities and
a quartic model identity; the conditional
probability argument and all-order limit
are not formalized end to end.
The independent script
\path{certificates/xi_high_order_pair_concentration.py}
tests the predicted decrease of the
pair-sum variance on two numerical meshes
and uses exact rational arithmetic for
the quartic identity.
The theorem should be compared with
the established derivative-aspect
Jensen-polynomial results
\cite{GORZ,JensenXi}; novelty is not
asserted without that comparison.
