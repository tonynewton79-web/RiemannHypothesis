\begin{abstract}
The Jacobi theta kernel gives an exact Fourier representation
of the Riemann $\Xi$-function, but familiar positivity properties
of the kernel need not determine where the transform vanishes.
We investigate this distinction using differential-concavity
iterations, generalized Laguerre expressions and heat-deformed
theta approximations. An exact fifth-iterate certificate
invalidates a proposed infinite kernel-concavity strengthening;
explicit positive kernels show that any fixed number of
Laguerre inequalities can miss nonreal zeros.
For the genuine theta series, modular inversion cancels every
odd endpoint derivative, while finite-mode truncations lose
that cancellation. We prove explicit Fourier approximation
bounds on the complex strip relevant to zeta zeros and extend
them uniformly over the de Bruijn--Newman heat interval.
Four bounded disks around classical real zeros admit
reproducible interval/Rouch\'e certificates. The heat-flow
Laguerre hierarchy has a coupled negative source when
evolved toward $t=0$; an exact double-zero example prevents
a generic scalar comparison from controlling it.
These results sharpen the approximation and proof barriers,
but do not establish global all-order Laguerre positivity
or the Riemann hypothesis.
\end{abstract}
