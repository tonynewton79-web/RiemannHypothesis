\begin{abstract}
We investigate three distinct questions concerning the Riemann $\\Xi$
Fourier kernel: differential-concavity iteration, generalized
Laguerre inequalities, and theta-lattice truncation.
The fifth iterate of the genuine theta kernel has a rigorous
negative interval certificate; explicit Gaussian kernels show
that no fixed finite Laguerre depth can certify real zeros.
A theta-specific incomplete-gamma resummation yields explicit
uniform-in-real-frequency derivative and Laguerre truncation
bounds. More sharply, Jacobi modular inversion cancels all odd
endpoint derivatives of the complete theta kernel, whereas every
fixed square-lattice cutoff leaves a positive first-derivative
defect and therefore an eventually negative first Laguerre
expression. This explains a quantitative arithmetic cancellation
not captured by generic curvature. Lean certifies algebraic
gates but not the complete theta Fourier proof; RH remains open.
Priority and independent review remain unresolved.
\end{abstract}
