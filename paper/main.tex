\begin{abstract}
The Fourier analysis of the Riemann theta kernel is
studied through exact differential certificates,
generalized Laguerre inequalities and finite
square-lattice approximations. A certified fifth-iterate
obstruction rules out an infinite kernel-concavity
approach, while Gaussian examples show that neither
radial curvature nor arbitrarily many finite Laguerre
tests determine the real-zero property.
The Jacobi modular functional equation gives exact
cancellation of all odd endpoint jets; each finite
theta-mode cutoff loses that cancellation and eventually
violates the first Laguerre inequality.
We establish explicit Fourier derivative approximation
bounds throughout a complex strip containing all
potential off-critical Xi zeros, and derive an effective
Rouch\'e criterion and an RH-equivalent compact
zero-migration condition. Gaussian smoothing repairs
parity and high-frequency Laguerre signs without moving
zeros. Exact-arithmetic tests, independent numerical
replays and Lean subtheorems support the stated
analytical scope. Neither global zero migration
nor RH is proved.
\end{abstract}
