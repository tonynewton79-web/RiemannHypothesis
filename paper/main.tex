\begin{abstract}
The Riemann hypothesis asks whether all nontrivial zeros of the
zeta function lie on its critical line. In the classical
Jacobi-theta representation, the main analytical difficulty is
that positivity of the underlying kernel need not control the
zeros of its Fourier transform. We investigate this distinction
through differential-concavity iterations, generalized Laguerre
inequalities, theta-lattice approximations, and the
de Bruijn--Newman heat flow. An exact interval certificate
invalidates a proposed infinite kernel-concavity hierarchy;
explicit positive kernels show that arbitrarily many finite
Laguerre inequalities cannot ensure real zeros. The monotone
curvature property of the classical theta potential was already
proved by Newman; our numerical certificate is an independent
quantitative verification, not a novelty claim. We establish
complex-strip truncation estimates and use interval arithmetic
to certify four previously known simple real zeros. For the
coupled heat-flow Laguerre hierarchy, a closed-slab comparison
requires zero simplicity, whereas a preterminal comparison
permits endpoint-singular coefficients and is sufficient for
RH without that additional assumption. The latter condition
is itself equivalent to RH by classical results and remains
unproved by theta-specific estimates. A validated full-heat-time compact rectangle proves the first
nontrivial finite-mode spatial source inequality for the genuine
theta series, at Laguerre order one and small Fourier frequencies.
This result does not supply the all-order, all-frequency bound.
Related division-free heat-penalty identities clarify the
remaining global proof barrier. A quantitative inverse-zero-gap
argument establishes a necessary logarithmic-square spatial
coefficient, with leading lower threshold $1/8$ at heat time
$t=1/2$; no corresponding global upper envelope is proved.
At a hypothetical nondegenerate collision in the heat slab,
we additionally prove the sharp inverse-time coefficient
$3/2$ and a nonintegrable comparison singularity;
this does not establish any collision for the Riemann theta flow.
We do not prove RH or extend the known
finite-height verification frontier.
\end{abstract}
