\begin{abstract}
The Jacobi theta kernel gives an exact Fourier representation
of the Riemann $\Xi$-function, but familiar positivity properties
of the kernel need not determine where the transform vanishes.
We investigate this distinction using differential-concavity
iterations, generalized Laguerre expressions and heat-deformed
theta approximations. Our curvature analysis builds on
Newman's GHS-based theta-potential convexity theorem (1991),
not a new claim of curvature monotonicity. An exact fifth-iterate certificate
invalidates a proposed infinite kernel-concavity strengthening;
explicit positive kernels show that any fixed number of
Laguerre inequalities can miss nonreal zeros.
For the genuine theta series, modular inversion cancels every
odd endpoint derivative, while finite-mode truncations lose
that cancellation. We prove explicit Fourier approximation
bounds on the complex strip relevant to zeta zeros and extend
them uniformly over the de Bruijn--Newman heat interval.
Four bounded disks around classical real zeros admit
reproducible interval/Rouch\'e certificates. The coupled heat-flow Laguerre hierarchy reveals a
precise global obstruction: the actual heat transform
$H_{1/2}$ has increasingly dense simple real zeros,
forcing its adjacent-order Laguerre ratio to grow without
bound. Thus a previously proposed height-independent
comparison cannot hold even at the known real-zero time.
A spatial parabolic comparison theorem shows that
local boundedness of the comparison coefficient suffices;
no global logarithmic-square height envelope is required.
An exact inverse-gap calculation shows that this spatial
condition is equivalent, using the classical heat-flow
theorems, to RH together with simplicity of every zeta zero.
A division-free Duhamel inequality avoids division at multiple
or nearly colliding zeros. A division-free constant-penalty Duhamel criterion also
works at multiple zeros. A new quantitative positive-part
identity establishes an unconditional, spatially uniform
$O(C^{-1})$ approximation to the heat-smoothed negative
Laguerre mass. It reduces the sufficient RH target from
vanishing unscaled penalty to boundedness in the penalty
parameter, without proving that bound for the genuine
theta family.
The Duhamel criterion can in fact be tested at a single
Fourier point for every Laguerre order. A quantitative
negative-witness estimate explains why possible high-frequency
violations can be exponentially difficult to detect from
that central observation. These are exact equivalences and
conditional bounds, not a verification of RH.
At the central Fourier frequency we prove that the
integrated source consumes a strictly increasing fraction
of the initial heat budget, tending to all of it at high
Laguerre order; a uniform fractional source margin is impossible.
Its two-frequency source decomposition exposes a failure of
spectral pointwise comparison after Fourier inversion.
The actual theta correlation decays super-Gaussianly,
excluding a proposed positive Gaussian-mixture factorization.
A further paired-zero construction shows that the classical
zero-density law cannot itself yield the proposed logarithmic-square
spatial Laguerre comparison: microscopic zero separation remains
an independent theta-specific requirement.
For the genuine theta family, fixed-difference Fourier correlation
slices change sign at known simple real zeros and have a negative
Fourier symbol near zero, excluding a proposed pointwise-positive
or convolution-square decomposition before Laguerre weighting.
The fully weighted square-lattice decomposition now shows that
individual diagonal mode-pair Fourier contributions at every
odd Laguerre order are negative at sufficiently high frequency,
even at a heat time where the full transform is real-rooted.
Jacobi modular parity cancels their combined leading coefficient;
this exact compensation is necessary but does not yet establish
nonnegativity of the complete Fourier correlation.
RH and zero simplicity remain unproved.
\end{abstract}
